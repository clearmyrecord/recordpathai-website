Tennessee Second Chance Initiative

Exploring a carefully limited, court-reviewed expungement pathway for qualifying older felony aggravated stalking convictions, while preserving protections for survivors and public safety.

Proposal status: Policy concept only. No bill has been introduced or enacted through this initiative.

Read the proposalUnderstand current law

What Tennessee law currently says

Tennessee Code Annotated § 39-17-315(c) classifies aggravated stalking as a Class E felony; especially aggravated stalking is a Class C felony. Section 40-32-107(a)(1)(C) lists the Class E felonies eligible for ordinary conviction expungement, but aggravated stalking is not on that list. Section 40-32-107(a)(1)(D) also excludes misdemeanor stalking and protective-order violations.

Completion of a sentence and the passage of time do not, by themselves, establish ordinary expungement eligibility for felony aggravated stalking or for excluded misdemeanor convictions. The exact conviction subsection, offense date, case history, and current law matter.

Tennessee also enacted Public Chapter 719 in 2026, expanding the availability of judicial expungement consideration following a governor's pardon. That is a separate, case-specific process; it does not create automatic expungement for misdemeanor stalking.

A proposed judicial-review pathway

The initiative would explore a new judicial petition procedure or narrow exception to § 40-32-107 for qualifying older felony aggravated stalking convictions, with separate consideration of related misdemeanor stalking and protective-order convictions.

  • Focus on qualifying Class E felony aggravated stalking convictions, not Class C especially aggravated stalking, with individualized consideration of any threats, violence, and safety risks.
  • Require a substantial waiting period after completion of all felony sentence requirements; an initial policy concept is ten years.
  • Permit judicial review of the conduct, rehabilitation, subsequent history, and public safety.
  • Allow the district attorney to respond, and provide safe, confidential means for a protected person to submit relevant input.
  • Keep existing protection orders fully enforceable; expungement would not erase them or override related restrictions.
  • Preserve access to nonpublic records where authorized by law, including appropriate future criminal-justice use.

A felony conviction does not establish the conduct was free of threats or danger. These are proposed safeguards for discussion, not enacted eligibility rules or a promise of relief.

Common questions

Can someone file under this proposal now?

No. The proposed judicial-review pathway would require legislation. Existing expungement, pardon, and other possible relief should be assessed under current law.

Does this proposal legalize repeated unwanted contact?

No. It addresses potential record relief years after a conviction. Stalking laws and court orders would continue to apply.

Will RecordPathAI guarantee eligibility?

No. RecordPathAI is not a court or law firm. Eligibility depends on the offense, judgment, timing, and applicable law.
