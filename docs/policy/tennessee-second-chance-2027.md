# RecordPathAI — Tennessee Second Chance Initiative
**Draft policy brief for legislative counsel and stakeholder review — November 2026**
**Status:** proposed reform only; not existing eligibility law; no introduced bill

## Public policy question
Should Tennessee create a discretionary, court-supervised route to expungement for certain older misdemeanor stalking convictions that remain excluded under Tenn. Code Ann. § 40-32-107(a)(1)(D), while maintaining protections for people subjected to stalking?

## Current legal baseline
- Tenn. Code Ann. § 40-32-107(a)(1)(D)(xxi) excludes stalking under § 39-17-315 from ordinary misdemeanor expungement.
- Tenn. Code Ann. § 40-32-107(a)(1)(D)(iv) also excludes violation of a protective or restraining order under § 39-13-113(g).
- The general five-year waiting period for *eligible* misdemeanors does not override offense exclusions.
- Public Chapter 719, enacted in April 2026, expanded the potential expungement route following a governor's pardon. The eligibility and procedure for that route require independent legal review.
- Distinguish **expungement** (Tennessee term often "expunction") from any separate sealing or confidentiality measure. This concept targets public conviction record relief, not termination of protection orders.
- Consult current official statutory publications and the 2026 enacted session law; the public 2025 codification may not reflect every 2026 amendment.

## Proposed policy design — not bill text
**Eligible offense class:** only certain misdemeanor § 39-17-315 convictions, with a separately considered provision for misdemeanor § 39-13-113(g) convictions arising from the same episode. The latter must be explicitly reviewed and may be treated differently because it concerns violation of a court order.

**Initial proposed threshold:** ten years after completion of sentence; no new stalking or protective-order-violation convictions; compliance with current orders; sentence obligations fulfilled; petitioner has established a rehabilitation record.

**Exclude from this particular pathway:** violence causing injury, credible threats, weapons, conduct involving minors or sexual violence, aggravated/especially aggravated stalking, and repeated post-conviction stalking. Legislative counsel should determine objective definitions and interaction with offense elements.

**Judicial discretion:** noticed hearing and evidence-based, individualized findings; prosecutor may object; safe and confidential opportunity for protected-person input without mandatory confrontation or direct contact; public-safety assessment; written reasons for grant/denial; no automatic relief.

**Record safeguards:** continuing enforceability of any civil protective order, no authorization to contact protected persons, retention and authorized access to sealed/nonpublic justice records as required by law. Draft in harmony with § 40-32-108 and applicable reporting rules.

**Temporal scope:** potential applicability to qualifying convictions predating enactment; review constitutional, administrative, and judicial process issues.

## Questions counsel must resolve
1. Does an exception within § 40-32-107(a), or a new standalone subsection, avoid conflicts with the statute's one-time-expungement and other-ineligible-convictions restrictions?
2. How should convictions for both stalking and protective-order violation from one episode be treated?
3. What objective evidence determines whether the conduct included a threat, violence, or serious risk even when the charge is a misdemeanor?
4. How will protected-person privacy and notification operate?
5. What fees, TBI certification, review standards, appeals, post-order access and reporting obligations apply?
6. How does the 2026 pardon-expungement change affect these cases and language?
7. Can a pilot or reporting requirement generate reliable statewide statistics without identifying protected people?

## RecordPathAI implementation boundaries
- Separate **current-law eligibility** from **potential legal reform**, clearly labeled across public pages and product screens.
- A conviction on the excluded list must **not** be automatically labeled eligible for ordinary expungement.
- A pardon path can be displayed as **seek qualified legal review**, never as guaranteed eligibility.
- Do not charge for a Tennessee stalking expungement packet or activate a packet generator based on this proposal.
- Only add Tennessee-specific automated rule changes after enacted law, attorney verification, dated rule versioning, test cases, and staging QA.
- Maintain user's existing architecture rules: authoritative record data only in Supabase; do not use browser-local state to silently populate legal forms; secure protected records and do not collect protected-person contact details through campaign pages.
- Distinguish a public policy interest form from court-case intake; if later created, use consent, privacy notice, minimal data collection, and no automatic enrollment in political outreach.
- Preserve the RecordPathAI core 3-step eligibility-to-packet flow; advocacy sits within Resources, not on the primary conversion route.

## Stakeholder review
Invite input from Tennessee criminal defense and expungement counsel, prosecutors, survivors and victim service advocates, court clerks, legislators, and researchers. Keep anonymous aggregate data and documented case outcomes separate from personal narratives and do not publish unverified impact statistics.
