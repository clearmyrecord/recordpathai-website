# RecordPathAI — Tennessee Second Chance Initiative
**Revised policy brief — November 2026**
**Status:** proposed reform for legislative and Tennessee counsel review; not enacted legislation; not an eligibility determination.

## Corrected focus
Create a narrow, discretionary, court-supervised expungement route for certain older **felony aggravated stalking** convictions, particularly qualifying Class E offenses under Tenn. Code Ann. § 39-17-315(c), while maintaining safeguards for protected persons and public safety. The initial version focused on misdemeanors; this document expressly corrects that scope.

## Present legal baseline
- Tenn. Code Ann. § 39-17-315(c)(2): aggravated stalking is a Class E felony; § 39-17-315(d)(2): especially aggravated stalking is a Class C felony. The felony classification can reflect different aggravating circumstances; do not infer the precise subsection or conduct from a general label.
- Under § 39-17-315(c)(1)(E), knowing contact in violation of a protective order is one possible factor in felony aggravated stalking; it is **not** a finding about any person's specific judgment.
- § 40-32-107(a)(1)(C) enumerates Class E felony convictions that may qualify for ordinary expungement; aggravated stalking is not on that list. § 40-32-107(a)(1)(D) excludes misdemeanor stalking and misdemeanor protective-order violations.
- The normal five-year wait for some *listed eligible* Class E felony convictions does not, on its own, give Class E aggravated stalking ordinary expungement eligibility.
- Tennessee's 2026 Public Chapter 719 (SB 2197/HB 2452) broadened court consideration of expungement **after a governor's pardon**, with specified offense exclusions and public-safety review. It is separate from ordinary conviction expungement; do not suggest a pardon or expungement is guaranteed.
- For older convictions, analyze the statute as of the offense and judgment, plus current expungement provisions and intervening amendments. Tennessee counsel must verify all citations against official current law before outreach or publication.

## Proposed eligibility and safeguards — policy concept, not enacted rules
1. **Primary covered offense:** certain Class E felony aggravated stalking convictions; consider related misdemeanors separately. Do not include Class C especially aggravated stalking in the initial concept.
2. **Review of actual conduct:** assess whether the offense involved physical injury, credible threats, a deadly weapon, minors, sexual violence, or other serious safety risks. Text messages alone do not establish either eligibility or absence of harm.
3. **Waiting period:** initial proposal of ten years after completed sentence, including probation, assessments, restitution, and other legally required obligations.
4. **Subsequent history:** require meaningful evidence of rehabilitation and no new stalking or protective-order-violation convictions, plus continuing compliance with any court orders.
5. **Judicial discretion:** petition in court of conviction; hearing and written findings on rehabilitation, underlying circumstances, protected-person safety, and public interest.
6. **Procedural protections:** prosecutors may object; protected individuals can give confidential input safely without requiring direct engagement or confrontation with the petitioner.
7. **Order independence:** record relief must not terminate or weaken any valid protective order, no-contact restriction, or other court order.
8. **Record retention:** preserve appropriate nonpublic criminal justice access and any legally required reporting despite public expungement.
9. **Retroactivity:** legislative counsel should consider whether qualifying past convictions may petition after effective date, subject to constitutional and administrative review.

## Questions for Tennessee legislative counsel
- Is a new § 40-32-107 subsection preferable to adding Class E aggravated stalking to § 40-32-107(a)(1)(C)? How do § 40-32-108 and one-time limits interact?
- How precisely to distinguish Class E aggravated stalking from Class C especially aggravated stalking, and from misdemeanor § 39-13-113(g) convictions in a single course of conduct?
- What factors should be bright-line bars versus discretionary considerations?
- What protected-person notice, confidentiality, court forms, costs, standard of proof, appeal, record-access and review mechanisms are necessary?
- How does Public Chapter 719's pardon-based process interact with this proposed independent procedure?
- Can aggregate data be evaluated without exposing individuals, stalking survivors, or confidential records?

## RecordPathAI implementation guardrails
- Separate **current law** and **proposed law**; mark ineligible convictions accurately, including the felony class and cited subsection.
- Do not transform a conviction of felony aggravated stalking into a misdemeanor for screening, marketing, payment, or filing.
- Do not make eligibility, packet generation, checkout, court filing, or premium entitlements available on the basis of this proposed statute.
- Display the separate 2026 pardon review possibility only as information to discuss with qualified Tennessee counsel.
- Follow existing product architecture: court details are stored in Supabase, not silently sourced from browser local storage; access is secure and minimised.
- Keep policy participation distinct from criminal-case intake, with informed consent and no collection of protected-person contact details on the campaign page.
- Preserve the main 3-step RecordPathAI screening funnel; place noncommercial advocacy education in Resources.

## Evidence & outreach
Seek documented sources for anonymized charging, conviction, case disposition, completed sentence and post-conviction outcomes. Do not publish personal case allegations without verified records or imply no harm solely because messages contained no explicit threats. Seek input from Tennessee defense lawyers, prosecutors, court officials, service providers for stalking survivors, civil liberties organizations, and legislators. Outreach may be drafted but is **not authorized to send without the user's specific approval of exact recipients and message**.
